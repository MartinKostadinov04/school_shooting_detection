# The desk-as-gun problem is solved by stacking, not by switching detectors

**Stop trying to make YOLO discriminate a desk from a pistol on its own — the peer-reviewed literature has been explicit since 2018 that single-stage appearance-only detectors will produce high-confidence false positives on visually similar handheld objects, and the consensus solution is a multi-stage pipeline rather than a better backbone.** The two research groups who have published most on this exact failure mode (University of Granada — Olmos, Tabik, Pérez-Hernández, Lamas, Herrera; and VISILAB/UCLM — Ruiz-Santaquiteria, Velasco-Mata, Vallez, Bueno, Deniz) report **22.5–87.2% false-positive reduction from autoencoder filtering**, **80% from temporal LSTM verification (MULTICAST)**, and **+4 to +18 AP from pose-aware two-stage cascades** — all stacked on top of an off-the-shelf detector. For a school-safety GPU deployment with labeled data, the architecture choice matters less than the verification stack you wrap around it. The leading detector candidates are YOLOv8/v10/v11 for speed (120+ FPS) or RT-DETRv2 / Cascade-Swin for accuracy on small, occluded CCTV guns; transformer-based detectors are now demonstrably ahead on CCTV imagery but only by ~3–5 AP, and they cost 3× the inference time. The bigger leverage is in training data construction and the post-processing layers below.

## What the 2020–2025 architecture benchmarks actually show

YOLO variants dominate the literature (~70–80% of papers), with **Faster R-CNN serving as the high-accuracy two-stage baseline** and SSD/RetinaNet as middle references. The most rigorous head-to-head comparisons are Bhatti et al. 2021 (*IEEE Access* — 7 architectures on custom CCTV, YOLOv4 best F1≈91%), Salido et al. 2021 (*Applied Sciences* — RetinaNet AP 96.36% beat YOLOv3 and Faster R-CNN on Monash Gun), Hsueh & Yang 2025 (*MDPI AI* — six YOLO versions on 17k Roboflow handguns, mAP@0.5 0.9791–0.9838 with **YOLOv8x best on mAP@0.5:0.95 = 0.872**), Vijayakumar et al. 2023 (YOLOv4 mAP 96.04% at 19 FPS beat Faster R-CNN), and Kambhatla & Ahmed 2024 (Faster R-CNN 89% AP > YOLOv5 78%, but at one-tenth the speed).

The single most important architecture paper is **Yellapragada et al.'s "CCTV-Gun" benchmark (CVPR-W 2023, arXiv:2303.10703)**, which evaluated Faster R-CNN, Deformable DETR, DetectoRS, Cascade Swin-T, and ConvNeXt-T on three merged real-CCTV datasets with intra- and cross-dataset protocols. Two findings cut through the noise: **transformer-backbone two-stage detectors (Swin-T, ConvNeXt-T) beat ResNet-50 Faster R-CNN by 3–5 AP on small/occluded handguns**, and **cross-dataset mAP collapses by 60%+ regardless of architecture**. A model trained on mock-attack data and tested on real-crime UCF footage drops to 4–12% AP@0.5. This is the most important number in this entire literature: high published mAP scores are dataset artifacts, not deployment indicators.

**Are transformers beating YOLO?** Conditionally yes on accuracy, no on speed. RT-DETR-R50 hits 53.1 AP / 108 FPS on COCO, edging YOLOv8 in both. Bustamante & Gutiérrez (*CLEI EJ* 2024/2025) showed **RT-DETRv2 + BiFPN beats YOLOv10 with >97% precision** on MGD/USRT/UCF, running on Jetson AGX Xavier. PDGV-DETR (2025, MDPI) reports mAP@0.5 = 85.9% beating YOLOv5/8/10/11, baseline RT-DETR, DINO, and Deformable DETR on a violent-conflict scene dataset. **Co-DINO/Co-DETR has not been benchmarked on firearm data in any peer-reviewed paper** — a genuine research gap as of April 2026.

## The false-positive reduction techniques that actually work

The literature has converged on five quantitatively validated FP-reduction strategies, listed in order of reported effectiveness. **First, hard-negative dataset enrichment**: Olmos & Tabik's foundational 2018 *Neurocomputing* paper reformulated the problem as FP minimization, showed that simply adding 28 confusable classes (phones, pens, white backgrounds) collapsed FPs from many to five; Pérez-Hernández et al. 2020 packaged this into the **Sohas dataset** with explicit pistol/knife/smartphone/bill/purse/card classes. **Second, temporal multi-frame confirmation**: Olmos et al.'s **MULTICAST (arXiv:2104.11653)** chains a Faster R-CNN detector → CNN spatial confirmation → LSTM trajectory verifier and reports an **80% reduction in false alarms** versus single-image baseline. **Third, scene-specific autoencoder filtering**: Vallez et al. 2021 (*NCAA*) train a deep autoencoder on the FPs a deployed detector produces in the target scene, then filter detections by reconstruction error — **22.5–87.2% FP reduction with 1.2–47% precision improvement**. **Fourth, pose/hand-region constraint** (covered below). **Fifth, two-stage CNN binary verification** of detector bounding boxes (Pérez-Hernández et al.'s ODeBiC; Berardini et al. 2024).

These methods are additive. Layering hard negatives + binary verifier + pose check + temporal smoothing is exactly the design pattern the Granada and VISILAB groups have published repeatedly with cumulative improvements.

## Person-holding-gun is a distinct and better-defined problem

Several papers explicitly distinguish "gun in the scene" from "person holding a gun" and demonstrate that the latter framing reduces FPs by leveraging human context. **Basit et al. 2020 (ICIP, arXiv:2005.09329)** is the seminal "firearm carrier" paper: a two-stage HOI pipeline detects guns and people, enumerates person-firearm pairs, builds a paired bounding box, and runs a CNN with Adaptive Average Pooling to classify carrier vs. non-carrier — **AP 78.5% on extended firearm-carrier data**, and explicitly addresses the failure of naive IoU pairing in crowded scenes. **Lamas et al. 2022 (*Neurocomputing* 489) — WeDePE** is the cleanest cascade: pose estimator runs on the full frame, an "Adaptive Pose Factor" computes the optimal hand-region crop size based on body-camera distance, hand crops are tiled into a composed image, and a weapon detector runs only on those crops; reaches **94.4% precision on Sohas**. **Ruiz-Santaquiteria et al. 2021 (*IEEE Access*)** dual-branch combines hand-region image with a binary pose image, **+4.23 to +18.9 AP over the previous best pose-based approach**; the 2023 *Pattern Recognition* extension adds DeiT/ViT features and an explicit FP-filter sub-network. **Flores et al. 2024 (SPoTem)** adds an LSTM over pose-keypoint sequences and finds pose-keypoint temporal sequences outperform spatial-feature sequences for video. The 2025 arXiv:2503.12215 paper combines YOLOv8s + MediaPipe with a normalized hand-to-gun distance threshold for "Threat" classification, reporting mAP@0.5 = 0.98 on a 9,500-image augmented dataset.

A critical caveat from Batista et al. 2026 (*Springer LNCS* 15993): in real urban CCTV where assailants deliberately maintain natural postures, **pose-based gains shrink** — the published +18 AP figures are on curated test sets where actors visibly brandish weapons. Expect 10–20% lower numbers in genuine deployment.

## Datasets and the cross-dataset reality check

The dataset landscape is dominated by two ecosystems: the **Granada/DaSCI public corpus** (Pistol 3k images, Knife 2k, Sohas detection 5,859 images with 6 classes including hard-negatives, plus 9k+ classification sets) and the **CCTV-perspective benchmarks** (Monash Gun MGD 2,857 images / 250 videos at 512×512; US Real-Time/Mock Attack USRT from University of Seville with 5,149 images of mock attacks in an educational center; UCF-Crime handgun frames 1,616 images of real crimes; CCTV-Gun aggregating all three with cross-dataset protocols and similar-object attribute tags). For a school deployment, the **University of Seville's USRT is the closest real-world analogue** because it was actually shot inside an educational center with mock attackers and includes smartphone hard-negatives.

The cross-dataset numbers from CCTV-Gun are sobering. Intra-dataset Swin-T + Faster R-CNN reaches handgun AP@0.5 = 91.7 on MGD; trained on MGD+USRT and tested on UCF, the same model drops to **6.8 AP**. Deformable DETR drops from 89.3 (MGD intra) to 11.7 (UCF cross). This means that **a model trained on web/movie images or even on clean mock-attack data will not work on your school's CCTV stream without on-site fine-tuning**. Hnoohom et al. 2022 also showed that **2×2 image tiling (SAHI-style) gave a 10× mAP improvement on small CCTV guns** — the single highest-leverage augmentation in the literature.

Auxiliary corpora worth merging: **YouTube-GDD** (5,000 images, 16,064 gun instances + 9,046 person instances), **Linksprite Qi et al. 2021** (~51k images with deliberate hard negatives), **Ruiz-Santaquiteria 2024 firearm-action dataset** on Mendeley (398 videos labeled Handgun/Machine_Gun/No_Gun across walking/jogging/sneaking/holding-phone/drinking-water — the most school-relevant action-aware corpus), and **Unity synthetic data** from Seville for domain randomization. Avoid relying on IMFDB-derived movie stills as a primary source — they bias the model toward cinematic lighting and full visibility.

## Recommended architecture for a school GPU deployment

Build a layered pipeline rather than swapping detectors. The fastest-impact changes are at the bottom of this list and are individually validated in the literature:

- **Tier-1 always-on detector at 30+ FPS:** YOLOv8m or YOLOv10s for speed-accuracy balance (Hsueh & Yang 2025), or RT-DETRv2 if you can afford ~40 FPS for ~3–5 AP on small-object accuracy. Train on combined USRT + MGD + UCF + Sohas with **explicit smartphone, wallet, hairdryer, remote, water-bottle, and desk negatives**, plus 2×2 SAHI tiling for small guns.
- **Tier-2 verification cascade triggered by Tier-1 hits:** MediaPipe Hands or YOLOv8-pose extracts hand keypoints; require any positive gun box to overlap a hand region within an adaptive pose-distance factor (Lamas 2022 WeDePE) — this single rule plausibly eliminates the desk-as-gun and hairdryer FPs. Add a small EfficientNet-B0 binary "gun vs. not-gun" classifier on the cropped bbox (Pérez-Hernández 2020 ODeBiC pattern).
- **Tier-3 temporal verification:** require k-of-n consecutive frame detections with consistent IoU tracking before alerting (MULTICAST — 80% FP reduction). A static detection on a desk with no motion and no associated hand should never alarm.
- **Tier-4 person-holder pairing:** adopt Basit et al. 2020's HOI pairing so the alarm names the carrier — operationally critical for school lockdown UX.

Specifically address the 62%-confidence-on-a-desk failure: **threshold tuning alone will not fix this, because YOLO confidence is poorly calibrated on small datasets and high-confidence FPs are documented as the norm, not the exception** (Olmos 2018, Salazar González 2020). The pose-overlap constraint is the most direct fix — a "gun" with no hand within 2× the bbox diagonal is automatically a desk, a phone, or a hairdryer. Combine with hard-negative training that includes labeled desk and classroom-furniture backgrounds collected from your actual cameras.

## Conclusion: the field's strongest signal

The most important lesson from five years of peer-reviewed firearm-detection research is that **dataset construction and verification stacking dominate architecture choice**. A YOLOv8 with proper hard negatives, pose-overlap filtering, temporal smoothing, and person-pairing will outperform a state-of-the-art Cascade Swin-T trained on poorly curated data. Real-CCTV deployment readiness is gated by cross-dataset generalization, which collapses to 4–12% AP for every architecture without on-site fine-tuning — meaning any school deployment must include a data collection phase capturing FPs from the actual cameras and adding them as labeled negatives. Treat industry products like ZeroEyes and Omnilert as cautionary tales (DHS 2023, StateScoop 2024 documented multiple false-alarm lockdowns), and pitch the system as "rapid response and situational awareness" rather than "active shooter prevention." The published path forward is clear: stack the layers, evaluate on cross-dataset CCTV, and build the verification cascade that the Granada and VISILAB groups have spent six years validating. Co-DINO benchmarking on firearms remains an open research gap if novelty is desired, but the deployable answer today is a layered YOLOv8/RT-DETR cascade with pose, temporal, and binary-classifier verification — not a single, perfectly tuned detector.
